| 1 | Multi-Dataset Plant Leaf Disease Detection (2025) | EfficientNet-B0, EfficientNet-B3, ResNet50, DenseNet201 (fine-tuned) | PlantDoc + web-sourced plant images (real-world, uncontrolled backgrounds) | Fine-tuned multiple pretrained CNNs; added Gaussian noise augmentation to improve generalisation | Compare pretrained CNNs on messy, real-world (not lab) images | EfficientNet models generally outperformed the others on the combined dataset | Lab-trained models don't generalize to real field photos - exactly the gap a phone-camera project must solve |

| 4 | A Comprehensive Review on Leaf Disease Detection Using Deep Learning (Mustofa et al., 2023) | ViT, DCNN, CNN, YOLO, RSNSR-LDD/DDN, hybrid CNN+ViT (35 papers, PRISMA) | PlantVillage, PlantDoc, Cassava, Wheat Rust, Rice Leaf Disease, Tomato, Sugarcane, Olive | PRISMA-based systematic review, 3-stage screening | Compares ViT vs CNN approaches across crops | ViT models generally edged out CNNs by a small margin, at far higher compute cost | ViT annotation is expensive and gains were often only 1-3% over CNN - a real cost-benefit gap |
| 5 | Machine Learning for Leaf Disease Classification: Data, Techniques and Applications (Yao et al., University of Tasmania) | Shallow ML vs. Deep Learning vs. Augmented/Transfer Learning | ~15 public datasets including PlantVillage, PlantDoc, Maize Leaf NLB, JMuBEN coffee, Cassava, Rice Diseases | Survey of ~100 papers, categorised by technique family | Compares transfer-learning results directly | Transfer learning consistently outperformed training from scratch, up to 99.56% on PlantVillage | No standard train/val/test split across studies; explainability is called out as a largely missing piece |
| 6 | PD36-C: A Compact 1.251 Million Parameter CNN Model for Plant Disease Detection | Custom lightweight CNN (1.251M parameters) | Case-study plant disease image set (limited detail available) | Small-footprint architecture design for constrained hardware | Model compression for offline deployment | Demonstrates a sub-1.3M-parameter model can still function as a usable classifier | Abstract-level detail only; full text was not accessible for deeper review |
| 7 | Deep Learning-Based Disease Detection in Potato and Mango Leaves (Mishra et al., 2025, Scientific Reports) | Baseline CNN, AlexNet, ResNet, EfficientNet (all fine-tuned, ImageNet, Adam optimizer) | PlantVillage Potato (2,152 images) + Kaggle Mango (4,000 images) | 224x224 resize, augmentation, 80:20 split, grid-searched hyperparameters | Benchmarks 4 architectures under identical protocol plus 4-stage ablation | EfficientNet best at 97.8% test accuracy, minimal overfitting | Authors' own error analysis found 2.2% of errors from domain shift and poor image quality - real field-readiness gap |
| 8 | Multi-Class Plant Leaf Disease Detection with Mobile App Integration (Foysal et al., 2024) | Custom 9-layer CNN vs. Bayesian-SVM+RF, YOLOv5+Swin, VGG16, AlexNet+GAP | Kaggle 'New Plant Diseases Dataset' - 87,867 images, 14 crops, 26 classes | 256x256 resize, augmentation, TensorFlow Lite conversion for mobile | Deploys via TensorFlow Lite into an offline Android app | 99.7% training accuracy, 98.14% validation accuracy, beat all compared models | App only names the disease - no severity level or treatment dosage, no IoT/drone integration |
| 9 | Plant Disease Segmentation Networks for Fast Automatic Severity Estimation (Zhao et al., 2025, MDPI Agriculture) | PDSNets (LinkNet + ResNet-18 encoder), 3 sizes, vs. U-Net, DeepLabv3+, LinkNet | Self-built field dataset: soybean bacterial blight, wheat stripe rust, cedar apple rust | Pixel-level 3-class annotation, severity = disease-pixels / (disease+healthy pixels) x100 | Benchmarks segmentation networks on accuracy-vs-speed trade-off | Smallest version: 2.9M params, 91.96% F1, running at 34.18 images/sec on CPU | Explicitly states the model has not been deployed on mobile platforms; lab-to-field domain gap still causes major F1 drops |

## Research Gap Synthesis

Across all 10 papers, classification-only work (rows 1-5, 7, 8) never adds severity
scoring. Severity-focused work (rows 9-10) uses heavier models and covers only one
disease each. Lightweight/mobile-deployment work (rows 6, 8) never adds severity or
explainability. No paper combines disease classification, severity scoring,
explainability, and treatment recommendation in a single lightweight, deployable
pipeline — this is the specific gap CropDoc addresses.
